Stress 35: In the window

A choose-your-own-adventure about exam stress

The Midterm

How to play
  • Pick what you'd do. Each choice changes your stress, shown as the boat on the river at the top.
  • The goal is to stay in the window: some stress helps, too much or too little hurts.
  • Decisions are timed. If time runs out, the game picks for you.
  • Keys 1 to 4 pick choices in the order shown. Click the text or press Space to show it all at once.
Settings

Reduce effects and heartbeat sound are at the bottom of the page and can be changed during play.

About this project

NSCI 140 Group 3: Téa Carrigg, Aishvihn Litt, Thomas Wood, Dirgh Patel

The target audience of this project would be university and college-aged students. Many students face stress and anxiety during exams, and despite it being so normal, students do not know how the body’s release of cortisol and its stress responses impact the brain regions, particularly the brain regions that are critical for memory, recall, and focus. Our main focus will be acute exam stress and its effects on working memory and long-term memory retrieval. The core goal of this project is to explore why students struggle during exams even when they feel that they understand the material, and to help them feel curious and empowered about the conditions under which they best learn. Roy et al. (2023) found that memory scores dropped, quite significantly, closer to exams (around 76% down to 63%), and that cortisol was negatively related to both memory performance and parahippocampal activity.

Predicted impact: Over time, this awareness can help students feel more confident and in control of how they study and perform, and more open to finding strategies that work best for them.

Roy, T., Saroka, K. S., Hossack, V. L., & Dotta, B. T. (2023). The effects of exam-induced stress on EEG profiles and memory scores. Behavioral Sciences, 13(5), 373. https://doi.org/10.3390/bs13050373
